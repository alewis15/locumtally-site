How the calculator works

A W-2 salary and a 1099 hourly rate aren’t comparable until you account for three things: the extra payroll tax a contractor pays, the benefits an employer pays for, and the weeks you don’t bill. The calculator does it in four steps.

  1. Take-home value of the W-2 job. Salary, minus your employee share of Social Security (6.2% up to the wage base) and Medicare (1.45%), plus the benefits you’d have to replace: health insurance, disability and life insurance, and the employer retirement match.
  2. Self-employment tax. As a 1099 contractor you pay both halves: 15.3% on 92.35% of your net profit, with the 12.4% Social Security part capped at the 2026 wage base of $184,500 and the 2.9% Medicare part uncapped. The calculator finds the profit that leaves the same take-home value after this tax.
  3. Business costs. Malpractice, CME, licenses, dues and other business costs are added on top. They’re deductible business expenses, so they come off your profit before self-employment tax. Health insurance, disability and retirement contributions don’t lower self-employment tax, so they’re paid out of your net.
  4. Billable hours. The 1099 gross you need is divided by the hours you’ll actually bill: hours per week × (52 − weeks off).

The reverse mode runs the same steps backward. It starts from a 1099 rate and finds the W-2 salary that leaves you with the same take-home value, including the retirement match you’d get on that salary.

Worked example

With the default inputs, a $210,000 W-2 CRNA job with 6 weeks off, $8,000 of health insurance, a 4% match and $2,000 of disability and life cover is worth $213,916 a year in take-home value before income tax. To match that on 1099, you need $243,310 of profit (self-employment tax is $29,394), plus $7,500 of business costs, for a gross of $250,810. Over 1,840 billable hours, that’s about $136.31 an hour. The W-2 job pays $114.13 for each hour you actually work, so the 1099 rate needs to be roughly 19% higher just to break even.

2026 figures and sources

  • Self-employment tax rate: 15.3% (12.4% Social Security + 2.9% Medicare). Source: IRS, Self-employment tax.
  • 2026 Social Security wage base: $184,500. Sources: the IRS page above and the Social Security Administration contribution and benefit base table. SSA lists $192,300 for 2027.
  • 92.35% factor and the $400 minimum: self-employment tax applies to 92.35% of net profit, and only once net earnings reach $400 (IRS Schedule SE).

What this estimate leaves out

It compares pay before income tax, so federal and state income tax, the qualified business income deduction, the deduction for half of self-employment tax and the 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax on high earners aren’t included. These are roughly similar for both jobs, and some favor the 1099 side. It also doesn’t value things like tail coverage, unpaid gaps between contracts, credentialing time or the freedom to pick your schedule. Treat the result as a floor to negotiate from, not a final answer, and run your situation past a tax professional who works with clinicians.

How to become a 1099 CRNA

Most CRNAs who go 1099 do a few things first: set up a business (often a single-member LLC or PLLC, depending on your state), get an EIN, buy their own malpractice coverage, and line up credentialing with each facility or group. Then the work starts: tracking every shift, billing call and premium hours correctly, and invoicing on time. The 1099 CRNA time tracking guide covers the record-keeping, and the call pay calculator shows what a week of shifts and call is worth under your contract.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good 1099 CRNA hourly rate?

It depends on your market, call and the W-2 offers you’d be giving up. Rather than relying on a rule of thumb, enter a real W-2 offer in this calculator to see the 1099 rate that breaks even, then negotiate above it.

Is 1099 or W-2 better for a CRNA?

1099 usually pays more per hour and lets you deduct business costs, but you pay both halves of Social Security and Medicare, buy your own benefits and malpractice, and aren’t paid for time off. W-2 is simpler and steadier. The right answer is the one that leaves you ahead after those costs, which is what this calculator estimates.

How much is self-employment tax in 2026?

15.3% of 92.35% of your net profit: 12.4% for Social Security on up to $184,500 of earnings, plus 2.9% for Medicare on all of it. A 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax applies above $200,000 (single) or $250,000 (married filing jointly).

Why does the 1099 rate need to be so much higher?

Three reasons: you pay the employer’s half of payroll taxes, you buy the benefits an employer would have paid for, and you’re only paid for the hours you work, not for vacation, holidays or CME days.

Does this calculator include income tax?

No. It compares pay before income tax, which keeps the comparison fair and simple. Your income tax depends on your whole household, deductions and state, so ask a tax professional for that part.
