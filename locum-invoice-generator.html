How to use the invoice generator

  1. Enter your business details exactly as they appear on the W-9 you gave the facility. Billing offices match the name on the invoice to the vendor record.
  2. Fill in Bill To with the facility’s legal name and the accounts payable contact. The person who signs your timesheet is rarely the person who pays you.
  3. Add the PO or contract number if the facility uses one. Many won’t process an invoice without it.
  4. Add one line per shift. Type hours as a decimal (10.5) or as hours and minutes (10:30). Put premium hours, call stipends and called-in time on their own lines at the rates in your contract.
  5. Add reimbursable expenses such as parking, mileage or lodging, and attach the receipts when you send the invoice.
  6. Download the PDF and email it to the billing contact. The due date is worked out from your payment terms in calendar days.

Every amount is hours × rate, rounded to the nearest cent, half a cent up. When you type hours as hours:minutes, the amount is worked out from the exact minutes, so 10:20 at $175 is $1,808.33, not 10.33 × $175 = $1,807.75. That’s the same math the Locum Tally app uses.

What a locum invoice needs to get paid

There’s no official IRS invoice form for 1099 work. An invoice is a business record that lets the facility match your work to your contract and pay you. The ones that get paid on time have the same things in common:

  • A unique, sequential invoice number (LT-0012, LT-0013 and so on) and a real due date, not just “Net 30”.
  • A service period and one line per shift, with start and end times in the description if your contract pays by the hour.
  • Premium and call pay on separate lines, so accounts payable can check each one against the rate sheet.
  • Clear payment instructions. Leave your Social Security number off. The facility already has your taxpayer ID from your W-9.
  • No patient information, ever. Case details don’t belong on an invoice.

Frequently asked questions

Is this locum invoice generator really free?

Yes. There’s no sign-up, no watermark and no limit on invoices. It’s made by Locum Tally, an iPhone app for 1099 clinicians, as a free tool.

Is my information sent anywhere?

No. The invoice and the PDF are created in your browser. Nothing you type leaves your device. If saving is on, the invoice is stored only in this browser’s local storage, and you can clear it any time.

Can I use it as a 1099 invoice generator for non-locum work?

Yes. It works for any independent contractor who bills by the hour, the shift or a flat fee. Change the descriptions to suit your work.

How do I bill call stipends and premium hours?

Put each on its own line. A call stipend is quantity 1 at the stipend amount. Premium hours are the hours past your contract’s threshold at the premium rate. Called-in time is the hours you worked while on call at your called-in rate.

Should I type hours as decimals or hours and minutes?

Either works. 10.5 and 10:30 are the same. If your contract bills in increments, such as 15 minutes, round the shift first and enter the billed time.

What payment terms should I use?

Use the terms in your contract. Net 30 is common for facilities. Net 15 or Net 7 gets you paid sooner if the facility agrees. The generator writes the actual due date on the invoice.
