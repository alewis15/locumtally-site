1099 CRNA time tracking: hours, call pay and rounding

Updated November 19, 2026 · General information, not tax or legal advice

As a 1099 CRNA or locum anesthesia provider, your hours are your invoice. If the record is fuzzy, the invoice is too, and the money you lose is usually small amounts on many shifts: a missed callback, a rounding rule applied the wrong way, a premium hour that never got billed.

What to record for every shift

  • Facility and, if you work at more than one, which contract the shift falls under.
  • Start and end time, recorded when they happen rather than reconstructed at the end of the week.
  • Unpaid breaks, if your contract deducts them.
  • Call: whether you were on call, the call window, and the exact times you were called in.
  • Anything billable outside the shift, such as an approved expense, with its receipt.

Your hours record is a business record, not a clinical one. Don't log patient names, MRNs or case details. You never need them to bill your time.

Contract terms that change the math

Locum anesthesia contracts vary a lot. Before your first shift, find these terms in your agreement and write them down:

TermWhat it meansQuestion to answer
Hourly rateBase rate for regular hoursIs it the same at every site under this contract?
Premium or overtimeA higher rate after a set number of hoursAfter how many hours: per shift, per day or per week?
Weekend and holiday ratesDifferent rates on certain daysWhich holidays count?
Call payA flat stipend, an hourly call rate, or bothIs called-in time paid on top, and at what rate?
Guaranteed hoursA minimum you're paid per shift, even if sent home earlyPer shift or per day? At the base rate?
Billing incrementHow time is rounded: actual minutes, or 6, 15 or 30 minutesRounded up, or to the nearest increment?
Payment termsWhen the facility must payNet 15, Net 30? Weekly or monthly invoices?

How rounding changes a shift

Take one sample shift: 6:52 AM to 4:01 PM with a 30-minute unpaid break, at $175/hour. That's 9 h 09 min on site and 8 h 39 min worked. Here's what the same shift bills under different increments:

Sample numbers. Rounding applied to worked time after the break.
Billing incrementBilled timeAmount at $175/h
Actual minutes8 h 39 min$1,513.75
6 minutes, rounded up8 h 42 min$1,522.50
15 minutes, nearest8 h 45 min$1,531.25
15 minutes, rounded up8 h 45 min$1,531.25
30 minutes, rounded up9 h 00 min$1,575.00

The spread on this one shift is $61.25. Over a year of shifts that adds up, in either direction. Bill exactly the way your contract says, apply it to every shift the same way, and you'll rarely get a question from the billing office.

Guaranteed hours work the other way. If your contract guarantees 8 hours per shift and you're released after 5 h 20 min, you bill 8 hours: the 5 h 20 min worked plus a 2 h 40 min top-up, usually at the base rate. Check which rate your contract uses.

Habits that keep the record clean

  • Clock in when you start, not when you remember. A Lock Screen button or widget makes this a two-second habit.
  • Log call-ins the same night. Called-in times are the easiest hours to lose.
  • Check the week before you invoice. Compare your hours with the facility's schedule or sign-in record.
  • Invoice from the record, not from memory. Each invoice line should trace back to a recorded shift.
  • Back it up. Your hours are part of your business records. The IRS asks you to keep records that support your income (IRS recordkeeping guidance).

Paper, spreadsheet or app?

A notebook works until you have two facilities with different rules. A spreadsheet handles the math if you build the formulas for premium hours, call and rounding, but you still have to type every time in and out. Staffing-agency apps track hours for that agency's assignments only.
