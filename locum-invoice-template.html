Locum invoice template for locum tenens providers

Updated November 19, 2026 · General information, not tax or legal advice

If you work locum tenens as a 1099 contractor and bill the facility yourself, every invoice needs the same handful of details. Here's a template you can copy, a filled-in example, and the mistakes that slow payment down.

What to include on a locum invoice

There's no official IRS invoice format. A locum invoice is an ordinary service invoice, and the facility's accounts payable team mostly needs to match it to your contract and pay it. Include:

  • Your name or business name, exactly as it appears on the W-9 you gave the facility, plus your address, email and phone.
  • Bill To: the facility's legal name, the billing contact or accounts payable department, and their address or billing email.
  • Invoice number (unique and sequential, for example LT-0012), invoice date and due date. Write the actual date, not just "Net 30."
  • PO or contract number, if the facility uses one. Many billing offices won't process an invoice without it.
  • Service period, for example Nov 2–8, 2026.
  • One line per shift: date, start and end time, unpaid break, billed hours, rate and amount.
  • Premium and call lines on their own rows: hours past your premium threshold, call stipends, called-in time and weekend or holiday rates, each at the rate in your contract.
  • Reimbursable expenses on separate lines with receipts attached, if your contract covers them.
  • Total due and how to pay (ACH details, check address or a payment portal).

Leave off: your Social Security number (the facility gets your taxpayer ID from your W-9) and any patient information. Case logs and patient details never belong on an invoice.

Tips to get paid faster

  • Ask for the billing contact on day one. The person who signs your timesheet is rarely the person who pays invoices.
  • Invoice on a schedule. Weekly or at the end of each assignment, matching your contract. A late invoice is a late payment.
  • Match the facility's records. If they keep timesheets or anesthesia records with times, make sure your hours agree before you send.
  • Use the same rounding every time. If your contract bills in 15-minute increments, round every shift the same way and say so. Our time tracking guide shows how increments change the total.
  • Track what's outstanding. Note when each invoice was sent, when it's due and any partial payments, then follow up a few days after the due date.
  • Keep a copy. The IRS lists invoices among the records that support your gross receipts (IRS: What kind of records should I keep).
